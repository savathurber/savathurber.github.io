Biodegradable Gas Generation System for Autonomous Marine Propulsion

I designed and experimentally tested a passive oxygen-generation system for marine-robot propulsion, targeting pressurized gas delivery without mechanical actuation.

Project Summary

How can a biodegradable marine robot generate useful propulsion pressure without a conventional power system?

The project developed a passive gas-generation mechanism for underwater propulsion. My teammates and I investigated hydrogen-peroxide decomposition with yeast and iterated through release mechanisms to improve pressure generation, gas delivery, and reliability while using water-soluble and biodegradable materials.

Engineering Workflow

Iterative design and testing

Each design-build-test cycle addressed practical limits in reaction control, release geometry, sealing, and delivery of gas to the propulsion subsystem.

01 / VALIDATE

Reaction feasibility

Tested hydrogen peroxide and yeast as a gas-generation chemistry.

02 / RELEASE

Capsule concepts

Evaluated staged delivery using gelatin capsules.

03 / GEOMETRY

Flow mechanisms

Investigated funnel and spiral geometries for controlled release.

04 / REFINE

Washer stack

Developed a weighted, water-soluble paper mechanism.

05 / INTEGRATE

Propulsion test

Integrated the final mechanism with the propulsion team's system.
